# Goals

The planner's charter: what every change and every place in the queue serves, in order of priority, and the rubric that places each item. Every queue decision in the lane queues, `docs/queue-feature.md` and `docs/queue-backlog.md`, cites its tier and the data behind it: a benchmark, a CI time, a gate result, a token count, a wall time, a finding, or the user's request.

1. UI: a pleasant UI and UX first, in carat's Facet design language (calm, readable, carat's own personality, never a copy of another editor). The user's now-request of 2026-11-10 ("I want now a pleasant UI and UX as priority"): its UI items are T2, ahead of every T3 to T6 item and of the pipeline items, only T0 before them. Earlier, at `cursor-shapes`, the user put the UI first in this order: the buffer list; the bars (a global status bar with the mode pill and the reserved `lsp` slot, buffer tabs); the leader layout; floating windows; then, after code intelligence, the rest of the UI track, `markdown-preview`, `csv-viewer`, and `indent-guides`.
2. Code intelligence, second at the user's request: the language server client with diagnostics filling the bar's `lsp` slot; completion as you type (fuzzy matching, kind icons, the type on the right, Enter inserts and Tab replaces); the highlighted item's documentation beside the list, fetched lazily; K quick documentation in a scrollable popup with markdown rendered and a key to pin it into a split; parameter info; go to and peek definition; buffer-word completion with Ctrl-N and Ctrl-P. Requests and documentation text from a language server are untrusted: escape sequences are stripped before the terminal, requests are asynchronous and cancelled when stale, and a key pays no cost for them.
3. Security, never traded for the goals above: no merge without the security review of the committed diff and its result in the pull request, never skipped; a High or Medium finding at confidence 8 or above blocks the merge, and blocking security fixes go to the front of the queue (T0) whatever the order of the goals. At the end of each sprint of six merged changes (five before sprint-17), `sprint-planning` reviews the sprint's combined diff, and the whole codebase every second sprint; a delivery never runs that review itself.
4. Token and pipeline efficiency: a change delivered in few tokens and little wall time, CI kept fast, by a pipeline that never forgets and improves itself (the retrospective may also simplify the workflow or improve its script), with agents effective at writing code. The user made this goal 1 in `pipeline-speed`, when a change took about an hour and 1.1M to 1.7M total tokens, and moved it below the UI and code intelligence at `cursor-shapes`: pipeline items yield to features (the rubric).
5. Performance at run time, kept by the benchmark and a history of benchmark results.
6. Code quality the Ruby way, within Spinel's rules.
7. Clean Architecture that is easy to extend.
8. Code simple and elegant for the people who read it.
9. Neovim command support.
10. The README kept current, including how carat is built with AI.
11. Documentation size and quality controlled.

## The rubric

Every item of a lane queue names its tier and its size, as `(T2 user request, estimated small)`, and every queue decision cites its tier and a number; `make docs-lint` refuses an item without both.

- Tiers, in order: T0 a blocking High or Medium security finding at confidence 8 or above, a red `main`, or a crash; T1 pipeline defects and pipeline efficiency (tokens, wall time, rework, CI time); T2 user-reported bugs and now-requests; T3 Low security findings reachable from an untrusted project without extra user action; T4 other efficiency (run time, tests), ordered by cost of delay; T5 roadmap items and user features, in the order of the goals above; T6 other Lows and advisories, folded into a change of the same domain and queued alone only when five accumulate.
- Inside T3 to T6, order by value divided by size. A quick win may jump ahead within T4 to T6, never ahead of T0 to T3. Same-domain quick wins are batched into one change to share its fixed overhead, 1.1M to 1.7M total tokens a change before `pipeline-speed`.
- Sizes are total-token budgets, input plus output as the workflow's usage reports them, estimated from the lane histories `docs/metrics-feature.tsv` and `docs/metrics-backlog.tsv`: small under 500k and 25 minutes, medium under 1.2M, large under 2.5M. The history records each change's estimate beside its total and output tokens and its wall minutes, and the retrospective flags an overrun (`sh tools/run_state.sh budget CHANGE`).
- Size check before a change starts: a brief with more than about four separate fixes, or estimated large outside the roadmap, is split into balanced changes. `workflow-trim` bundled seven fixes and needed four quality rounds across two runs; `guard-allowlist`, three fixes with four folds, took 5 rework rounds and 860,176 output tokens.
- The planner runs when the queue needs a decision: a new finding, a user request, or a split; otherwise the next item is taken as written.
- Aging: an item records how often it was pushed back; at three it rises one tier or gets a written reason.
- Sprints: a sprint from sprint-17 on is 6 changes, 3 + 3: three features in `docs/queue-feature.md` and three backlog changes in `docs/queue-backlog.md`, a backlog change being a bug fix or a tidy-up of review findings, docs, or tooling (`SPRINT_SIZE` and `SPRINT_SIZE_FROM` in `tools/run_state.sh`); sprint-16 and earlier close at five. At most one of them is a pipeline change (the workflow, agents, CI, tools, process docs), only for a defect; T0 items are exempt, do not count, and always go first. The decisions behind it, kept as history: the user's of 2026-11-11 replaced the rule of at most one non-feature change per three features with a sprint of 5 changes; that of 2026-11-15, so broken windows (`docs/architecture.md` section 1) are planned, made it three features and two fixes, the pipeline change among them; that of 2026-11-16 made the features and the fixes two lanes, each lane's rows in its own history; that of 2026-11-17, for now, so a later sprint may revisit it, added the third backlog change from sprint-17 on, the one-pipeline-change rule and the exception rules staying as they were. Each sprint item is self-contained: its locations, why with its numbers, a fix outline, the acceptance check, and the headings of the backlog entries it covers, since no delivery opens the backlog; the rest of the queue is the pool the next sprint draws from, features first. A feature is a now-request of the user, a feature the user requested or scheduled, or a user-visible roadmap feature the user approved, such as `search-and-substitute`. A feature passes T1 to T6 items, which otherwise keep their order among themselves; it never passes a T0 item nor the track it depends on (code intelligence after floating windows; `markdown-preview` and `csv-viewer` after the UI track); when no feature is ready, a plan decision says so and the next non-feature item runs.
- Pace, price, and quality (the user's decision of 2026-11-11, its findings part that of 2026-11-18): about one merged change per hour of running time, reported per sprint in `docs/sprints.tsv`; budgets are tracked, never enforced, an overrun reported in the run's report and the planner's decisions but no run stopped for it; quality held: the security review never skipped, check and CI green, blocking findings fixed in the run, the contributor's own quality review before the archive making each finding a task (the user's decision of 2026-11-21, which took the quality reviewer out of the delivery; the sprint review is the independent check); advisories and security findings of any severity in the change's own files fixed in place in the same pull request, within its three rounds, when the fix stays in those files and adds no behavior, each a task of the change's tasks.md, then re-reviewed: the check and the security review run again on the new head, except after a round of its own offering no security finding whose fix changed only records-only paths, where neither reruns, the skip noted in the run state (`review-lite`); the rest, and any left at the last round, recorded in the change's findings file, none dropped, and never re-raised as blocking in a later round.
- A brief part, plan note, or proposal states the problem, its numbers, and the acceptance check, never the implementation, which the contributor chooses. In `workflow-trim` a blocking quality finding reversed the approach a brief part, a retrospective note, and a plan note had all prescribed.

## Critical changes

The planner names a change critical in `critical` of its plan, a rule letter and the path that triggers it, from this list; a critical run's contributor starts use the session's model at the session's effort, the rest the agent file's tier at high effort (see Calibration). The planner also writes it at the end of the queue item's parenthesis as `, critical LETTER PATH`, which a delivery that skips the planner reads. The run state stores the rule as `metric.critical`, so a calibration's comparison leaves critical runs out.

- a: a security item (T0, or a security finding in its tier or headings).
- b: an edit of the merge gate or the guards: `tools/rules.sh`, `tools/gates.sh`, `tools/scan_text.sh`, `tools/agent_guard.sh`, `tools/run_state.sh`, `.claude/workflows/`, `.claude/agents/`, `.github/workflows/`.
- c: the toolchain: `SPINEL_REF` in the Makefile, `tools/spinel/` (a Spinel bump).
- d: code reading untrusted input or guarding the editor: `carat/domain/buffer.rb` and the undo history, `carat/plugin_api.rb` and the plugin host's guarded path, the `.caratrc` parser, the JSON-RPC language gateway, the process and file drivers.
- e: estimated large (the workflow applies it itself).

Escalation: after a self-check mismatch (`self_check` of the contributor's result), a self-check of match from an implement or fix start that the next finished check contradicts by failing a stage (noted `self-check-mismatch` with the stage; of the refusals only those of the contributor's own tree count, tracked files differing from HEAD or untracked files spin compiles, an environmental one such as `CHECK_JOBS` not), a start with no result, a refused head (a done result whose `head` is not the lane's `git rev-parse --short=12 HEAD`, which the workflow reads with `tools/lane_facts.sh` in a start of its own, noted `head-refused` and restarted once, a second refusal stopping the run; a head differing only in form from a committed lane's, a placeholder, a short or full hash, or upper case, is taken from git, noted `head-form`, and escalates nothing), or a refused quality review (a done implement result without its `quality_review`, noted `quality-refused` and restarted once the same way), the run's later contributor starts use the session's model at the session's effort, recorded as an `escalation` note and `metric.escalated`.

## Calibration

`CALIBRATION` in `.claude/workflows/deliver-change.js` sets each kind of agent start's model tier (`agent`, the tier the agent file's `model:` line names, or `session`; rows before `contributor-tier-trial` call it `cheap`) and reasoning effort (`low`, `medium`, `high`, or `session`), and the history's `calibration` column records what each run used, beside its phase tokens and calls and its first passes.

- Reasons: mechanical starts (resume, record, checker, benchmark, merger) run at low effort, the three with an agent file on the cheapest tier, the checker passing first 21 times in the 22 rows before `agent-calibration`; judgment starts (planner, quality, security, close) keep the session's model and effort, the delivery's quality review passing first only 13 times in 35 before the contributor took it over; `quality` is now the pull request text check's profile.
- First move, at `agent-calibration`: the scouts from the session's effort to low, the largest mechanical cost left at a default (over 33,000 output tokens on 18 of those 22 rows, up to 116,958; plan plus scout 26 to 37 percent of a run's output on many). The record start's cheapest tier waits for an agent file to name it.
- Rule: one kind moves at a time. A move is kept when, over the next five rows with it, the gates' first-pass rates and the rework rounds are no worse than over the five rows before it; otherwise the planner queues its revert. Since `contributor-self-check` the contributor runs the check in its own session and fixes a failing stage there, so every keep rule here that reads `rework` reads it plus `self_fix`, the check runs after the first of each implement or fix start (`metric.self_fix` in the run state, beside `metric.rework`; no history column yet), and a run whose rework only moved into the session counts no better. The security reviewer never moves to a cheaper tier or a lower effort.
- Trial, at `contributor-tier-trial` (the user's request of 2026-11-18, with its own keep rule in place of the five-row one): the contributor alone moves to `agent/session`, its agent file naming the mid tier, while the planner, quality, security, close, and every sprint-planning start keep the session's model. It is kept only if over the next 6 deliveries the history's quality first pass (`quality` in `first_pass`, which no delivery records since `review-in-openspec`, so the rule judges without it), `rework`, CI fixes (the rows with `ci:0` in `first_pass`), and `total_tokens` are each no worse than over the last 10 rows before it; otherwise the planner queues its revert: the contributor back to `session/session`, its `model:` line removed, and tools-test's contributor tier check dropped.
- Trial, at `contributor-tier-routing` (the user's request of 2026-11-19): the contributor moves to `agent/high`, the mid tier at high effort, and a run named critical (Critical changes above), or one that escalated, uses `session/session` for its contributor starts. The trial's comparison counts the runs at the new profile and leaves the critical and escalated ones out (`metric.critical`, `metric.escalated`); its keep rule is the one above, over the next 6 such deliveries. It ended at `contributor-tier-medium` by the user's decision of 2026-11-20, before that comparison closed.
- Trial, at `contributor-tier-medium` (the user's tier decision of 2026-11-20): the contributor moves to `agent/medium`, the mid tier at medium effort; critical, large-estimate, and escalated runs keep `session/session` for their contributor starts. Over the next 6 non-critical deliveries it is compared on the quality first pass, the rework rounds, the total and weighted tokens, and the wall minutes against the high-effort rows #233 to #250 (quality first in 2 of 5, rework 1.8, weighted 2.10M, 114 minutes) and the mid tier at the session's effort, rows #224 to #229 (1 of 6, rework 2.3, weighted 1.67M, 87 minutes); it is reverted to `agent/high` if the quality first pass or the rework gets worse (since `review-in-openspec` no delivery records the quality first pass, so the rework alone).
- End, at `contributor-tier-high` (the user's decision of 2026-11-21, before the 6 deliveries): the contributor returns to `agent/high`, the mid tier at high effort, `medium` staying an allowed effort; critical, large-estimate, and escalated runs keep `session/session`. Two runs ran at medium: #262 (1.23M total, 1.28M weighted, the day's cheapest) skipped its self-review rule and hand-edited snapshots, costing a fix round, and #265 returned a placeholder head, escalated to the session's model, and paid for both (2.19M total). The mid tier at medium effort drops procedural steps late in a run, so high effort returns; no comparison is left open for the planner to judge.
