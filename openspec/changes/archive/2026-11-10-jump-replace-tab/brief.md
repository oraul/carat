## Part 1 of tmp/briefs/gd-replace-tab.md, as given

A jump is `gd`, `Ctrl-W gd` excluded (it opens a new tab page, queued as `definition-tab`), a pick-list choice of a definition, and Ctrl-O and Ctrl-I through the jump list. On a jump to a buffer that has no tab, the target takes the current buffer's place in the tab line, at the same position, and the buffer left goes unlisted (`:ls` hides it, `:ls!` shows it with `u`, as Neovim marks an unlisted buffer), still loaded with its cursor and undo history. A jump to a buffer that already has a tab selects that tab and changes no other. Ctrl-O back to A gives A the tab again in B's place by the same rule, so going back and forth never grows or shrinks the tab line. A buffer the user opened on purpose (`:e`, the finder, the explorer, `:b`) keeps its tab when a later jump starts from another buffer; only the buffer the jump leaves loses its tab. A modified buffer never loses its tab (a jump from it is already refused with E37 today), and neither does a buffer shown in another window. An option keeps Vim's behavior: `option.jumptab replace` (the default, the user's choice) or `keep` (every jumped-to file adds a tab, as now), a `.caratrc` line with its docs row. The `docs/compatibility.md` row says it differs from Neovim by default and why.

Acceptance: a table-driven test that fails before the fix: from A, `gd` to B in the project leaves one tab, B's, in A's position; Ctrl-O leaves one tab, A's; a jump to a buffer with its own tab selects it and keeps both; a buffer opened with `:e` keeps its tab when a jump leaves another buffer; a buffer shown in a second window keeps its tab; `option.jumptab keep` keeps both tabs as now; `:ls` and `:ls!` show the left buffer as Neovim does an unlisted one. The outside-target behavior of #96 (read-only file buffer) keeps working under both settings. Allocations per key and rounds at main's figures.
